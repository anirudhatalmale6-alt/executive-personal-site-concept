01 — Profile

A career spent
between two
languages

Helena Marchetti advises governments, sovereign funds and listed boards on the questions that sit awkwardly between the legal department and the strategy committee: what an export control actually forbids, what a screening regime will do to a deal timetable, and what a state is likely to do next.

She began in commercial arbitration, spent eight years as her country's representative to the World Trade Organization, and now divides her practice between counsel work, non-executive roles and a standing advisory brief to two multilateral institutions. She has appeared before three international tribunals, led four bilateral negotiating teams, and served on the expert panel that redrafted a regional framework on investment screening.

“Economic statecraft is not a branch of law and it is not a branch of finance. It is the discipline of knowing which one a government will abandon first.”

Her written work appears in the Global Counsel Review and the Journal of Economic Statecraft, and she is a frequent voice on broadcast coverage of trade and sanctions policy. She holds a doctorate in public international law and lectures annually on geoeconomics at two graduate schools.

02 — Professional services

Where I am useful

Six mandates that make up most of the work. Select one to read more.

  1. Interpretation and contingency planning around unilateral and multilateral measures: licensing strategy, counterparty exposure, divestment sequencing, and the board paper that explains the decision to people who will read it two years later.

  2. Pre-signing assessment across screening regimes, mitigation undertakings, and direct engagement with reviewing authorities. Most of the value is created before the term sheet, not after the filing.

  3. Tariff and subsidy exposure, safeguard proceedings, and the strategic question of whether a dispute is worth winning. Includes advocacy before international tribunals and appearances as an expert on public international law.

  4. Standing counsel to boards and investment committees: scenario work, country entry and exit, and the discipline of separating a headline from a change in policy. Delivered as a retained brief rather than a report.

  5. Confidential mandates where an institution needs a channel it does not formally own — bilateral talks, consortium formation, and the resolution of matters that would be damaged by becoming public.

  6. Conference keynotes, moderated panels, and off-the-record briefings for boards, investor groups and public officials. Topics and past engagements are listed below.
